\section{Error Localisation Strategies}
\label{sec:background-localisation}

\Cref{sec:introduction-localisation} motivated a decomposition of the global DWR estimator into computable local contributions. 
% Elementwise integration by parts (IBP) gives strong cell residuals, flux jumps, and boundary residuals, but its derivation depends on the differential operator and boundary conditions. 
% Partition-of-unity localisation distributes the global DWR estimator through locally supported functions \(\{\chi_i\}_{i=1}^{M}\) satisfying \(\sum_i\chi_i=1\). For example, in the constructions considered in \cite{Richter_Wick_2015,ThieleWick2024,R6}, the resulting quantities are naturally associated with nodes, degrees of freedom, or temporal elements. These PU contributions have a valid variational interpretation, but they are not generally tied to a geometric residual mechanism. In particular, since a single PU function may overlap several spatial elements and temporal interfaces, its contribution may combine different components of the residual. An additional redistribution or marking rule is therefore needed to convert the PU contributions into indicators for specific space--time mesh entities.
\yue{Differenet ways of localisation have been discussed in Introductino.}
The present construction pursues a geometrically resolved decomposition. It probes the weak residual with polynomial functions supported on individual cells, spatial facets, temporal faces, and, when present, mixed space--time ridges. The resulting coefficients separate the residual mechanisms according to their geometric support, while still being recovered directly from UFL evaluations of the weak residual in \texttt{Firedrake}. Thus no problem-specific strong residual or hand-derived flux-jump formula is needed. This approach is motivated by the stationary bubble--cone construction of Rognes and Logg \cite{Rognes_Logg_2013}; our contribution is its extension below to discontinuous Galerkin discretisations in time.


\subsection{Stationary Bubble and Cone Projection}
\label{sec:stationary-residual-recovery}

We first recall the stationary construction for the primal residual. The nonlinear adjoint-residual term is treated in \cref{sec:nonlinear-adjoint-residual-localisation}. For each \(K\in\mathcal T_h\), let \(\mathcal F(K)\) denote its facets. Rognes and Logg formulate the following assumptions \cite{Rognes_Logg_2013}:
\begin{description}
  \item[(A1) Global decomposition.] The global residual is a sum of cell-local functionals,
  \begin{equation}
    \rho(v)=\sum_{K\in\mathcal T_h}\rho_K(v).
    \label{eq:stationary-global-decomposition}
  \end{equation}

  \item[(A2) Local decomposition.] Each local residual has the form
  \begin{equation}
    \rho_K(v)
    =
    (R_K,v)_K
    +
    \sum_{F\in\mathcal F(K)}(R_{K,F},v)_F.
    \label{eq:stationary-residual-representation}
  \end{equation}

  \item[(A3) Polynomial representation.] The residual densities are piecewise polynomial. For chosen degrees \(p,q\),
  \begin{equation}
    R_K\in\mathbb P^p(K),
    \qquad
    R_{K,F}\in\mathbb P^q(F).
    \label{eq:stationary-polynomial-representability}
  \end{equation}
\end{description}
Assumptions (A1) and (A2) identify the residual-supporting entities, while (A3) determines whether the recovery is exact or a weighted projection.

For a \(d\)-simplex \(K\) with vertices \(\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{d+1}\) and associated barycentric coordinates \(\{\lambda_i^K\}_{i=1}^{d+1}\), the cell bubble is defined by 
\begin{equation}
  b_K\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}\lambda_i^K,
  \label{eq:recovery-cell-bubble}
\end{equation}
and satisfies $b_K|_{\partial K}=0$ by construction.
This means that \(R_K^B\in\mathbb P^p(K)\) can be recovered from solving the variational problem
\begin{equation}
  (R_K^B,b_K\phi)_K
  =\rho_K(b_K\phi)
  \qquad\forall\phi\in\mathbb P^p(K).
  \label{eq:stationary-cell-projection}
\end{equation}

For \(F\in\mathcal F(K)\), let
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal I_F\coloneqq\{i:x_i\in F\},
  \qquad
  \beta_{K,F}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in\mathcal I_F}\lambda_i^K.
  \label{eq:recovery-facet-cone}
\end{equation}
This cone function is the analogue of the bubble function on a facet. It is nonzero on the relative interior of \(F\), vanishes on every other facet, and remains nonzero inside \(K\).
To test the facet residual using functions defined on the cell, we introduce the polynomial extension operator
\[
E_{K,F}:\mathbb P^q(F)\longrightarrow\mathbb P^q(K),
\qquad
(E_{K,F}\psi)|_F=\psi.
\]
Thus, \(E_{K,F}\) maps a polynomial defined on the facet \(F\) to a polynomial on \(K\) with the same trace on \(F\). We write \(\widetilde\psi:=E_{K,F}\psi.\)
\pef{You need to introduce this operator, give it a name and say what it does.} Once the cell density \(R_{K}^B\) is recovered, the facet residual contribution \(R_{K,F}^B\in\mathbb P^q(F)\) is defined by solving the variational problem
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(R_{K,F}^B,(\beta_{K,F}|_F)\psi\bigr)_F
  =
  \rho_K(\beta_{K,F}\widetilde\psi)
  -(R_K^B,\beta_{K,F}\widetilde\psi)_K
  \quad\forall\psi\in\mathbb P^q(F).
  \label{eq:stationary-facet-projection}
\end{equation}
The subtraction removes the cell action seen by the cone. Under (A1)--(A3), these weighted mass problems uniquely recover \(R_K\) and \(R_{K,F}\) \cite{Rognes_Logg_2013}. When (A3) does not hold, the procedure computes polynomial approximations to the residuals.

\subsection{Space--Time Extension of the Residual Recovery}
\label{sec:space-time-residual-recovery}


We now consider the extension of this approach to time-dependent problems.
Let \(V\) be a spatial trial and test space. We now consider an evolution problem of the form
\begin{equation}
  m(\partial_tu(t),v)+\mathfrak a(u(t);v,t)=\ell(v;t)
  \qquad\forall v\in V,\quad t\in(0,T),
  \label{eq:abstract-evolution-problem}
\end{equation}
with \(u(0)=u_0\). Here \(m\) is the time-derivative pairing, \(\mathfrak a\) contains the remaining spatial and possibly nonlinear terms, and \(\ell\) is the load functional. Denote
\begin{equation}
  I_n=(t_{n-1},t_n],
  \qquad k_n=t_n-t_{n-1},
  \qquad Q_{K,n}=K\times I_n,
  \qquad \tau=\frac{t-t_{n-1}}{k_n}.
  \label{eq:space-time-cell}
\end{equation}
On \(I_n\), trial and test functions are time polynomials with values in a spatial finite-element space \(X_n\), and may be discontinuous at \(t_n\). Following the standard space--time dG notation \cite{Bangerth_Rannacher_2003}, we define
\begin{equation}
  U^{n+}\coloneqq\lim_{t\downarrow t_n}U(t),
  \qquad
  U^{n-}\coloneqq\lim_{t\uparrow t_n}U(t),
  \qquad
  \llbracket U \rrbracket^n\coloneqq U^{n+}-U^{n-}.
  \label{eq:dg-time-traces}
\end{equation}
For matching spatial spaces, the global dG semilinear form can be written as
\begin{align}
  \mathcal A(U)(W)
  \coloneqq{}&
  \sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{I_n}
  \bigl[m(\partial_tU,W)+\mathfrak a(U;W,t)-\ell(W;t)\bigr],\mathrm dt
  \notag\\
  &+\sum_{n=2}^{N}m(\llbracket U \rrbracket^{n-1},W^{(n-1)+})
  +m(U^{0+}-u_0,W^{0+}).
  \label{eq:global-dg-semilinear-form}
\end{align}
The discrete problem is \(\mathcal A(U_h)(W_h)=0\) for all discrete tests. Throughout this chapter we use the convention
\begin{equation}
  \rho(U)(W)\coloneqq-\mathcal A(U)(W).
  \label{eq:dg-residual-sign-convention}
\end{equation}

The extension of the stationary recovery is based on the following assumptions.

\begin{description}
  \item[(A1) Global decomposition.] The global residual is the sum of local space--time functionals,
  \begin{equation}
    \rho(U_h)(W)
    =
    \sum_{n=1}^{N}
    \sum_{K\in\mathcal T_n}
    \rho_{K,n}(U_h)(W).
    \label{eq:space-time-residual-decomposition}
  \end{equation}

To be more specific, we can define the incoming defect by
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{n-1}(U_h)\coloneqq
  \begin{cases}
    U_h^{0+}-u_0, & n=1,\\[0.3em]
    \llbracket U_h \rrbracket^{n-1}, & n>1.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:dg-incoming-defect}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \rho_{K,n}(U_h)(W)
  \coloneqq{}&
  \int_{I_n}
  \Big[
    \ell_{K,n}(W;t)
    -
    m_{n,K}(\partial_tU_h,W)
    -
    \mathfrak a_{K,n}(U_h;W,t)
  \Big]\,\mathrm dt
  \\
  &-
  m_{n,K}\Big(
    \delta_{n-1}(U_h),
    W(t_{n-1}^{+})
  \Big).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:complete-local-slab-residual}
\end{equation}

Note that if \(V_{n-1}\ne V_n\), the outgoing trace must first be transferred into \(V_n\):
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{n-1}(U_h)
  =U_h^{(n-1)+}-P_{n-1}U_h^{(n-1)-},
  \qquad n>1,
  \label{eq:transferred-dg-time-defect}
\end{equation}
where \(P_{n-1}:V_{n-1}\to V_n\). The transfer and its adjoint are discussed in more details in \cref{sec:slab-transfer-adjoint}.

  \item[(A2) Local decomposition.] On each space--time cell \(Q_{K,n}\), the residual admits a representation by contributions supported on the cell interior, spatial facets, temporal facets, and, when required by $m$, their intersections, as shown in \cref{fig:space-time-residual-entities}.
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \rho_{K,n}(U_h)(W)
    ={}&
    (R_{K,n}^{B,\mathrm{vol}},W)_{Q_{K,n}}
    +
    \sum_{F\subset\partial K}
    (R_{K,F,n}^{B,\mathrm{sf}},W)_{F\times I_n}
    \\
    &+
    (R_{K,n-1}^{B,\mathrm{tf}},W(t_{n-1}^{+}))_{K} +
    \sum_{F\subset\partial K}
    (R_{F,n-1}^{B,\mathrm{xt}},
      W(t_{n-1}^{+}))_{F}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:space-time-entity-representation}
  \end{equation}

\item[(A3) Polynomial representation.]
The residual densities belong to the selected recovery spaces,
\begin{align}
  R_{K,n}^{\mathrm{vol}}
  &\in Q_{K,n}^{p_x,p_t},
  &
  R_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{sf}}
  &\in Q_{F,n}^{q_x,q_t},
  \notag\\
  R_{K,n-1}^{\mathrm{tf}}
  &\in \mathbb P^{r_x}(K),
  &
  R_{K,F,n-1}^{\mathrm{xt}}
  &\in \mathbb P^{s_x}(F),
  \label{eq:space-time-polynomial-representability}
\end{align}
whenever the corresponding densities are present.
\end{description}

Assumption (A1) follows naturally when the weak form is assembled slab by slab from cell and facet integrals. (A2) records the geometric supports. Under (A3), problems recover a density exactly when it belongs to the selected polynomial space; when (A3) does not hold, polynomial approximations are recovered.


\paragraph{Volume recovery.}
We first define the temporal interior bubble and its tensor product with the spatial cell bubble from~\eqref{eq:recovery-cell-bubble} by
\begin{equation}
  b_n^t(\tau)\coloneqq 4\tau(1-\tau),
  \qquad
  B_{K,n}\coloneqq b_Kb_n^t.
  \label{eq:space-time-volume-bubble}
\end{equation}
The function \(B_{K,n}\) vanishes on every codimension-one face of \(Q_{K,n}\). In particular,
\[
  B_{K,n}(t_{n-1}^{+})
  =
  B_{K,n}(t_n^{-})
  =
  0,
\]
so the temporal-interface term in \eqref{eq:complete-local-slab-residual} vanishes under this test.

The recovered volume density \(R_{K,n}^{B,\mathrm{vol}}\in Q_{K,n}^{p_x,p_t}\) is computed by solving
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(
    R_{K,n}^{B,\mathrm{vol}},
    B_{K,n}\Phi
  \bigr)_{Q_{K,n}}
  =
  \rho_{K,n}(U_h)\bigl(B_{K,n}\Phi\bigr)
  \qquad
  \forall\Phi\in Q_{K,n}^{p_x,p_t}.
  \label{eq:space-time-volume-projection}
\end{equation}
All spatial-facet and temporal-endpoint contributions vanish under these test functions.

\paragraph{Spatial-facet recovery.}
For \(F\subset\partial K\), define
\begin{equation}
  C_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{sf}}
  \coloneqq
  \beta_{K,F}b_n^t.
  \label{eq:space-time-spatial-cone}
\end{equation}

Let
\(
  E_{K,F,n}:
  Q_{F,n}^{q_x,q_t}
  \longrightarrow
  Q_{K,n}^{q_x,q_t}
\)
be a fixed polynomial extension and write \(\widetilde\Psi\coloneqq E_{K,F,n}\Psi\). After the volume density has been recovered, we can compute \(R_{K,F,n}^{B,\mathrm{sf}}\in Q_{F,n}^{q_x,q_t}\) by solving
\begin{align}
  &\Bigl(
    R_{K,F,n}^{B,\mathrm{sf}},
    \bigl.C_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{sf}}\bigr|_{F\times I_n}\Psi
  \Bigr)_{F\times I_n}
  \notag\\
  &\qquad =
  \rho_{K,n}(U_h)
  \bigl(C_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{sf}}\widetilde\Psi\bigr)
  -
  \Bigl(
    R_{K,n}^{B,\mathrm{vol}},
    C_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{sf}}\widetilde\Psi
  \Bigr)_{Q_{K,n}}
  \notag
  \qquad \quad
  \forall\Psi\in Q_{F,n}^{q_x,q_t}.
  \label{eq:space-time-spatial-facet-projection}
\end{align}


\paragraph{Temporal-face recovery.}
To isolate the incoming dG interface contribution, we define
\begin{equation}
  C_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf},-}
  \coloneqq
  b_K(1-\tau),
  \label{eq:temporal-trace-cone}
\end{equation}
which satisfies
\begin{equation*}
  C_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf},-}(t_{n-1}^{+})=b_K,
  \qquad
  C_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf},-}(t_n^{-})=0,
  \qquad
  \left.C_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf},-}\right|_{\partial K\times I_n}=0.
  \label{eq:temporal-cone-traces}
\end{equation*}

For \(\chi\in\mathbb P^{r_x}(K)\), define the temporal-face test function
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\chi) \coloneqq
b_K(1-\tau)\chi,
\label{eq:temporal-recovery-test}
\end{equation}
which vanishes on every spatial facet and the outgoing temporal face \(K\times\{t_n\}\) of \(Q_{K,n}\). Hence, the local residual decomposition reduces to
\begin{equation}
\rho_{K,n}(U_h)
\bigl(\mathcal E_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\chi)\bigr)
=
\Bigl(
R_{K,n}^{\mathrm{vol}},
\mathcal E_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\chi)
\Bigr)_{Q_{K,n}}
+
\bigl(
R_{K,n-1}^{\mathrm{tf}},
b_K\chi
\bigr)_K .
\label{eq:temporal-cone-support-splitting}
\end{equation}
Once the recovered volume density \(R_{K,n}^{B,\mathrm{vol}}\) is available, we compute the recovered incoming temporal-face density \(R_{K,n-1}^{B,\mathrm{tf}}\in\mathbb P^{r_x}(K)\) by solving
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(
R_{K,n-1}^{B,\mathrm{tf}},b_K\chi
\bigr)_K
={}&
\rho_{K,n}(U_h)
\bigl(\mathcal E_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\chi)\bigr) \\
&-
\Bigl(
R_{K,n}^{B,\mathrm{vol}},
\mathcal E_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf}}(\chi)
\Bigr)_{Q_{K,n},
}
\qquad
\forall\chi\in\mathbb P^{r_x}(K).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:temporal-facet-projection}
\end{equation}


\paragraph{Possible mixed spatial--temporal ridge.}
A separate mixed recovery is required only if the complete residual contains a nonzero functional supported on \(F\times\{t_{n-1}\}\). Such a contribution does not normally arise if $m$ is the \(L^2\) inner product, but it can occur when $m$ contains spatial derivatives, as arises in the Benjamin--Bona--Mahony problem in \cref{sec:bbm-mixed-ridge-specialisation} below.

For \(\psi\in\mathbb P^{s_x}(F)\), let \(\widetilde\psi=E_{K,F}\psi\) be the fixed extension used in the stationary facet recovery and define
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  C_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{xt},-}
  &\coloneqq
  \beta_{K,F}(1-\tau),
  \\
  \mathcal{E}_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{xt}}(\psi)
  &\coloneqq
  C_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{xt},-}\widetilde\psi.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:mixed-ridge-test}
\end{equation}
The mixed density \(R_{K,F,n-1}^{B,\mathrm{xt}}\in\mathbb P^{s_x}(F)\) is consequently computed by solving
\begin{align}
  \bigl(
    R_{K,F,n-1}^{B,\mathrm{xt}},
    (\beta_{K,F}|_F)\psi
  \bigr)_F &=
  \rho_{K,n}(U_h)
  \bigl(\mathcal{E}_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{xt}}(\psi)\bigr)
  -
  \Bigl(
    R_{K,n}^{B,\mathrm{vol}},
    \mathcal{E}_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{xt}}(\psi)
  \Bigr)_{Q_{K,n}}
  \notag\\
  &-
  \Bigl(
    R_{K,F,n}^{B,\mathrm{sf}},
    \left.\mathcal{E}_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{xt}}(\psi)\right|_{F\times I_n}
  \Bigr)_{F\times I_n}
  \notag\\
  &-
  \Bigl(
    R_{K,n-1}^{B,\mathrm{tf}},
    \mathcal{E}_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm{xt}}(\psi)(t_{n-1}^{+})
  \Bigr)_K,
  \qquad
  \forall\psi\in\mathbb P^{s_x}(F).
  \label{eq:mixed-ridge-recovery}
\end{align}
This subtraction leaves only the contribution supported on \(F\times\{t_{n-1}\}\).


\subsection{Recovery of the Nonlinear Adjoint-Residual Functional}
\label{sec:nonlinear-adjoint-residual-localisation}

% Recall that we wish to localise the global DWR estimate~\eqref{eq:practical-nonlinear-estimator}. 
% The construction above recovers localised densities for the primal residual \(\rho(u_h)\). It can be used directly for both primal-residual contributions and the Galerkin orthogonality term. Once \(u_h\) and \(z_h\) are fixed, the adjoint-residual term \(\rho^\ast(u_h,z_h)(\cdot)\) is a linear functional of the primal variation. It can therefore be evaluated on the same test functions used above, but it requires a separate recovery of the linearised PDE and goal derivatives rather than reuse of the primal-residual densities.
Let
\begin{equation}
  e_u\coloneqq u_h^+-u_h,
  \qquad
  e_z\coloneqq z_h^+-z_h,
  \label{eq:nonlinear-dwr-discrete-errors}
\end{equation}
where $(u_h,z_h)$ are the low-order primal and adjoint approximations and $(u_h^+,z_h^+)$ are their enriched counterparts. Recall that we wish to localise the global DWR estimate
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{\mathrm{global}}
  =\frac12\rho(u_h)(e_z)
   +\frac12\rho^\ast(u_h,z_h)(e_u)
   -\rho(u_h)(z_h).
  \label{eq:localised-symmetric-nonlinear-dwr}
\end{equation}

Once $u_h$ and $z_h$ are fixed, introduce the two linear residual functionals
\begin{equation}
  \mathscr R^{\mathrm p}(v)
  \coloneqq\rho(u_h)(v),
  \qquad
  \mathscr R^{\mathrm a}(\varphi)
  \coloneqq\rho^\ast(u_h,z_h)(\varphi),
  \label{eq:two-recovered-residual-functionals}
\end{equation}
where, by the adjoint-residual definition in~\eqref{eq:nonlinear-adjoint-residual},
\begin{equation}
  \rho^\ast(u_h,z_h)(\varphi)
  =J'(u_h)(\varphi)
   -\mathcal A'(u_h)(\varphi,z_h).
  \label{eq:general-adjoint-residual-functional}
\end{equation}
Both maps in \eqref{eq:two-recovered-residual-functionals} are linear in their displayed arguments. We can therefore apply the bubble recovery developed above twice, once to \(\mathscr R^{\mathrm p}\) and once to \(\mathscr R^{\mathrm a}\). In the present implementation, the two reconstructions use the same entity-wise recovery spaces introduced in assumption~(A3) and the same local test families, but solve two independent problems for two different residuals. Note that the two recovery spaces could be enriched independently if necessary.

\subsection{Construction of the Space--Time Error Indicators}
\label{sec:space-time-local-indicator-construction}

We now combine the recovered densities with the DWR weights. For each entity
\begin{equation*}
  E\in\{\mathrm{vol},\mathrm{sf},\mathrm{tf},\mathrm{xt}\},
\end{equation*}
\pef{say what these abbreviations mean.}
where $\mathrm{vol}$ denotes the cell-volume entity $Q_{K,n}$, $\mathrm{sf}$ a spatial facet $F\times I_n$, $\mathrm{tf}$ the incoming temporal face $K\times\{t_{n-1}\}$, and $\mathrm{xt}$ the mixed space--time ridge $F\times\{t_{n-1}\}$, the recovered primal- and adjoint-residual densities are paired with \(e_z\) and \(e_u\), respectively, to define \(\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm p,E}\) and \(\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm a,E}\). The correction contribution \(\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm c,E}\) pairs the recovered primal-residual density with \(z_h\).

For example, the volume terms are
\begin{align}
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm p,vol}
  &\coloneqq
  \bigl(R_{K,n}^{\mathrm p,vol},e_z\bigr)_{Q_{K,n}},
  \notag\\
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm a,vol}
  &\coloneqq
  \bigl(R_{K,n}^{\mathrm a,vol},e_u\bigr)_{Q_{K,n}},
  \notag\\
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm c,vol}
  &\coloneqq
  \bigl(R_{K,n}^{\mathrm p,vol},z_h\bigr)_{Q_{K,n}}.
  \label{eq:nonlinear-dwr-volume-components}
\end{align}
The spatial-facet terms are
\begin{align}
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm p,sf}
  &\coloneqq
  \sum_{F\subset\partial K}
  \bigl(R_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm p,sf},e_z\bigr)_{F\times I_n},
  \notag\\
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm a,sf}
  &\coloneqq
  \sum_{F\subset\partial K}
  \bigl(R_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm a,sf},e_u\bigr)_{F\times I_n},
  \notag\\
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm c,sf}
  &\coloneqq
  \sum_{F\subset\partial K}
  \bigl(R_{K,F,n}^{\mathrm p,sf},z_h\bigr)_{F\times I_n}.
  \label{eq:nonlinear-dwr-spatial-components}
\end{align}
At a dG temporal interface, the adjoint residual depends on the primal variation through its jump. Evaluating this residual at the primal error \(e_u\) gives the error jump
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{n-1}(e_u)
  =
  e_u(t_{n-1}^{+})
  -
  P_{n-1}e_u(t_{n-1}^{-}).
  \label{eq:primal-error-incoming-defect}
\end{equation}
Thus,
\begin{align}
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm p,tf}
  &\coloneqq
  \bigl(R_{K,n-1}^{\mathrm p,tf},e_z(t_{n-1}^+)\bigr)_K,
  \notag\\
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm a,tf}
  &\coloneqq
  \bigl(R_{K,n-1}^{\mathrm a,tf},
        \delta_{n-1}(e_u)\bigr)_K,
  \notag\\
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm c,tf}
  &\coloneqq
  \bigl(R_{K,n-1}^{\mathrm p,tf},z_h(t_{n-1}^+)\bigr)_K.
  \label{eq:nonlinear-dwr-temporal-components}
\end{align}
Finally, when the mixed entity is present, the analogous terms are
\begin{align}
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm p,xt}
  &\coloneqq
  \sum_{F\subset\partial K}
  \bigl(R_{K,F,n-1}^{\mathrm p,xt},
        e_z(t_{n-1}^+)\bigr)_F,
  \notag\\
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm a,xt}
  &\coloneqq
  \sum_{F\subset\partial K}
  \bigl(R_{K,F,n-1}^{\mathrm a,xt},
        \delta_{n-1}(e_u)\bigr)_F,
  \notag\\
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm c,xt}
  &\coloneqq
  \sum_{F\subset\partial K}
  \bigl(R_{K,F,n-1}^{\mathrm p,xt},
        z_h(t_{n-1}^+)\bigr)_F.
  \label{eq:nonlinear-dwr-mixed-components}
\end{align}

For each entity, the nonlinear contribution is
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{K,n}^{E}
  =\frac12\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm p,E}
   +\frac12\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm a,E}
   -\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm c,E}.
  \label{eq:complete-nonlinear-entity-indicator}
\end{equation}
The space--time cell indicator is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{K,n}
  =\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{vol}}
   +\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{sf}}
   +\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{tf}}
   +\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{xt}}.
  \label{eq:complete-nonlinear-cell-indicator}
\end{equation}
For a linear DWR estimator using only $\rho(u_h)(z^\star)$, this definition reduces to the sum of the primal components without the factors one half and without the adjoint and correction parts.

Since the recovery matches each residual functional only on the selected finite-dimensional bubble--cone test space, exact global-local equality is not generally assumed. We therefore define
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\mathrm{loc}}
  \coloneqq
\sum_{n,K}\eta_{K,n},
\label{eq:localisation-gap}
\end{equation}
To assess the quality of localisation, we define the \emph{localisation effectivity index}
\begin{equation}
I_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{loc}}
  \coloneqq
\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{loc}}}{\eta_{\mathrm{glob}}},
\label{eq:localisation-effectivity}
\end{equation}
whenever \(\eta_{\mathrm{glob}}\neq 0\). Exact localisation gives \(I_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{loc}}=1\).
