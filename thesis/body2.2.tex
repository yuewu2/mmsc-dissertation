\section{One-dimensional Advection-Diffusion Equation}
\label{sec: exp-1d}

The preceding heat-equation experiments examined the behaviour of the localisation procedure for a fixed observation functional. We now consider a one-dimensional advection--diffusion problem for two related purposes.
First, the one-dimensional spatial domain permits the adaptive mesh to be displayed directly in the $(x,t)$ plane, so that spatial and temporal refinement can be observed simultaneously. Second, by keeping the primal problem and localisation procedure fixed while varying the location of the observation sensor, we isolate the influence of the goal functional, and hence of the associated dual weight, on the resulting refinement pattern.


\subsection{Moving-pulse Problem}

We consider the advection--diffusion equation
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t u
  - \varepsilon\,\partial_{xx}u
  + b\,\partial_xu
  = f
  \qquad
  \text{in }(0,1)\times(0,T),
  \label{eq:advection-diffusion-1d}
\end{equation}
subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
\begin{equation}
  u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0,
  \qquad t\in(0,T],
\end{equation}
and the initial condition
\begin{equation}
  u(x,0)
  =
  x(1-x)
  \exp\!\left[-\beta(x-x_0)^2\right].
\end{equation}
The forcing term $f$ is manufactured so that the exact solution is
\begin{equation}
  u(x,t)
  =
  x(1-x)
  \exp\!\left[
    -\beta\bigl(x-x_0-bt\bigr)^2
  \right].
  \label{eq:moving-pulse}
\end{equation}
The parameters used throughout the experiments are
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon=0.02,
  \qquad
  b=0.6,
  \qquad
  \beta=100,
  \qquad
  x_0=0.2,
  \qquad
  T=1.
  \label{eq:advection-diffusion-parameters}
\end{equation}

The solution represents a narrow concentration pulse transported from left to right while undergoing diffusion. Its nominal centre follows the trajectory
\begin{equation}
  x_c(t)=x_0+bt=0.2+0.6t
\end{equation}
and therefore reaches $x_c(T)=0.8$ at the final time. The factor $x(1-x)$ ensures compatibility with the homogeneous boundary conditions and causes the amplitude of the pulse to vary as it moves through the domain. Figure~\ref{fig:advection-diffusion-exact} shows the resulting space--time solution.

\begin{figure}[htbp]

  \caption{Exact solution of the 1D advection--diffusion problem. The dashed line indicates the nominal pulse trajectory $x=x_0+bt$.}
  \label{fig:advection-diffusion-exact}
\end{figure}


\subsection{Quantity of Interest and Adjoint Sensitivity}

To study the influence of the goal functional, we introduce a family of finite-width terminal detectors,
\begin{equation}
  J_{x_d}(u)
  =
  \int_0^1
  \psi_{x_d}(x)\,u(x,T)\,\mathrm{d}x,
  \qquad
  \psi_{x_d}(x)
  =
  \exp\!\left[
    -\left(\frac{x-x_d}{r_d}\right)^2
  \right],
  \label{eq:qoi-gaussian-family}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  r_d=0.05,
  \qquad
  x_d\in\{0.7,0.8,0.9\}.
  \label{eq:sensor-locations}
\end{equation}
The functional may be interpreted as the response of an optical or concentration sensor whose sensitivity is maximal at $x_d$ and decreases smoothly away from its centre.

The three detector locations probe physically distinct parts of the pulse at the final time. The detector at $x_d=0.8$ is aligned with the pulse centre, the detector at $x_d=0.7$ observes its trailing side, and the detector at $x_d=0.9$ observes its leading side. Although the primal equation is identical in all three experiments, these observations assign different importance to errors in different regions of space--time.

Each $J_{x_d}$ is linear in the state $u$. The corresponding formal adjoint problem is
\begin{equation}
  -\partial_t z
  -\varepsilon\,\partial_{xx}z
  -b\,\partial_xz
  =0
  \qquad
  \text{in }(0,1)\times(0,T),
  \label{eq:advection-diffusion-adjoint}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  z(0,t)=z(1,t)=0
\end{equation}
and terminal datum
\begin{equation}
  z(x,T)
  =
  J'_{x_d}(u)(x,T)
  =
  \psi_{x_d}(x).
  \label{eq:advection-diffusion-adjoint-terminal}
\end{equation}
Changing the sensor centre therefore changes the terminal condition of the adjoint. 
The terminal datum is most sharply localised at $t=T$. To understand how this sensitivity is propagated to earlier times, we introduce the reversed time variable
\[
  \tau=T-t.
\]
The adjoint equation then becomes
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\tau z
  -\varepsilon\,\partial_{xx}z
  -b\,\partial_xz
  =0,
  \qquad
  z(x,0)=\psi_{x_d}(x).
\end{equation}
Thus, when propagated backwards in physical time, the terminal Gaussian is transported to the left by the adjoint dynamics and simultaneously broadened by diffusion.

We (therefore) expect the influence of the detector location to be most clearly visible near the terminal time, where the three dual weights remain narrow and spatially separated. Further backwards in time, diffusion broadens the weights and increases their overlap. Since the primal residual is common to all three experiments, the resulting refinement patterns are expected to become more similar at earlier times while separating towards the three observation locations near $t=T$. This behaviour will be examined in the adapted space--time meshes below.


\subsection{One-dimensional Residual Localisation}
\label{sec:one-dimensional-localisation}

The residual localisation requires a minor modification in one spatial dimension. In higher dimensions, the localised residual generally contains cell-interior contributions and contributions supported on spatial facets. In one dimension, however, the facets of an interval are its two endpoints. The facet contribution therefore remains present, but is represented by point evaluations at the left and right endpoints rather than by integration over edges or faces of positive dimension.

On each space--time cell
\[
  K\times I_n,
  \qquad
  I_n=(t_{n-1},t_n],
\]
the local indicator is decomposed as
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{K,n}
  =
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{vol}}
  +
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{end}}
  +
  \eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{time}},
  \label{eq:1d-local-indicator-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{vol}}$ is the cell-interior contribution, $\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{end}}$ contains the spatial endpoint contributions, and $\eta_{K,n}^{\mathrm{time}}$ represents the residual at the left temporal interface of the slab.


\subsection{Results and Interpretation}
\textcolor{blue}{space time axis}

In this numerical test, the initial discretisation contains $12$ spatial cells and $8$ time slabs. The primal solution and numerical dual use CG1 in space and dG0 in time. The enriched dual uses the corresponding higher-order space--time approximation. A marking parameter \(\theta=0.6\) is used. A time slab is bisected when the fraction of its marked spatial cells is at least \(15\%\).

The final adapted meshes are compared in Figure~\ref{fig:advection-diffusion-final-meshes}. Changing the detector location modifies the shape and terminal destination of the refined space--time tube. For $x_d=0.7$, refinement follows a comparatively continuous diagonal path towards the trailing part of the pulse. For $x_d=0.8$, the refined region remains concentrated around the central pulse trajectory as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:advection-diffusion-exact}. For $x_d=0.9$, the late-time refinement is shifted further to the right and bends towards the leading part of the pulse. When traced backwards in time, the three refined regions return towards the common initial pulse location near $x_0=0.2$. This demonstrates that the terminal sensor affects through the backward propagation of the dual weight.
