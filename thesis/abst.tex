\begin{abstract}

Goal-oriented adaptive finite element methods aim to reduce the error in a prescribed quantity of interest by refining the discretisation where it matters most for that output. For time-dependent partial differential equations, this requires localising a global dual-weighted residual (DWR) error estimator in both space and time. This dissertation develops an automated localisation procedure that works directly with the weak variational formulation.

The method extends bubble--cone recovery to discontinuous-Galerkin time discretisations. It recovers separate contributions associated with space--time cell interiors, spatial facets, temporal interfaces, and, where required, mixed space--time ridges. These contributions form an automated solve--estimate--mark--refine workflow for space--time adaptation.

The framework is implemented in \texttt{Firedrake} and assessed for the heat, advection--diffusion, Benjamin--Bona--Mahony, parabolic \(p\)-Laplace, and incompressible Navier--Stokes equations. The numerical experiments assess the observed goal error, the agreement of the reconstructed local estimator with the global DWR estimator, and the resulting adaptive refinement behaviour. The results demonstrate that the proposed workflow can support practical automated goal-oriented space--time adaptivity.


\end{abstract}
