\section{Incompressible Navier–Stokes Equations}
\label{sec:cylinder-nonlinear-dwr}

This final experiment extends the automated space--time framework to a nonlinear mixed problem: incompressible Navier--Stokes flow past a cylinder. 
The benchmark geometry is taken from \cite{benchmark,reference_value}, while the tensor-product space--time discretisation follows Roth et al.~\cite{non-flow}. More specifically, we use discontinuous Galerkin discretisation in time, Taylor--Hood finite elements \cite{TaylorHood} for the velocity--pressure pair, and a divergence-free projection \cite{projection} to transfer the velocity between successive spatial meshes. The automated bubble-recovery localisation and the resulting local error indicators are those developed in this thesis.


\subsection{Problem and Quantity of Interest}
\label{subsec:cylinder-problem}

Let
\(
  \Omega=((0,2.2)\times(0,0.41))\setminus\overline{B_{0.05}(0.2,0.2)}
\)
be the channel-with-cylinder domain, $I=(0,T)$, and take $T=8$ and viscosity \(\nu=10^{-3}\). The boundary is decomposed into the inlet \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{in}}\), channel walls and cylinder \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{wall}}\cup\Gamma_C\), and outlet \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{out}}\). We solve velocity and pressure \(U=(\boldsymbol u,p)\) satisfying
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:cylinder-strong}
\begin{align}
  \partial_t\boldsymbol u+
  (\boldsymbol u\!\cdot\!\nabla)\boldsymbol u
  -\nu\Delta\boldsymbol u+\nabla p &= \boldsymbol 0
  &&\text{in }\Omega\times I,\label{eq:cylinder-momentum}\\
  \nabla\!\cdot\boldsymbol u &=0
  &&\text{in }\Omega\times I.\label{eq:cylinder-continuity}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The prescribed inflow is
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{in}}(y,t)
 =\left(
  6\sin(\pi t/8)\frac{y(0.41-y)}{0.41^2},0
 \right)^{T},
 \label{eq:cylinder-inflow}
\end{equation}
with no slip on \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{wall}}\cup\Gamma_C\) and the natural traction condition at the outlet.  This is the nonstationary cylinder benchmark used by Roth et al.~\cite{non-flow}.

For
\[
 V_g(t)=\{\boldsymbol v\in[H^1(\Omega)]^2:
 \boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol g(t)\text{ on the essential boundary}\},
 \qquad Q=L^2_0(\Omega),
\]
\pef{If you have an outflow boundary condition, the pressure space should be $Q = L^2(\Omega)$, not $Q = L^2_0(\Omega)$ (since the natural outflow boundary condition involves the value of the pressure on the boundary, the pressure is no longer undefined up to a constant). Double check your code to make sure you're not imposing the mean-zero constraint, it shouldn't be.}
the weak spatial operator is
\begin{align}
 a(U;\Phi)={}&
 ((\boldsymbol u\!\cdot\!\nabla)\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v)_\Omega
 +\nu(\nabla\boldsymbol u,\nabla\boldsymbol v)_\Omega
 -(p,\nabla\!\cdot\boldsymbol v)_\Omega
  +(\nabla\!\cdot\boldsymbol u,q)_\Omega,
 \label{eq:cylinder-spatial-operator}
\end{align}
where \(\Phi=(\boldsymbol v,q)\in V_0\times Q\). The complete weak problem adds \((\partial_t\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol v)_\Omega\) and the initial-condition term.

The quantity of interest is the time-mean drag on the cylinder,
\begin{equation}
 J(U)=\frac1T\int_0^T C_D(U(t))\,\mathrm dt,
 \qquad
 C_D(U)=20\int_{\Gamma_C}
 \boldsymbol e_x\!\cdot\!\bigl((-pI+\nu\nabla\boldsymbol u)
 \boldsymbol n_C\bigr)\,\mathrm ds,
 \label{eq:cylinder-goal}
\end{equation}
where \(\boldsymbol n_C\) points from the cylinder into the fluid. We use the reference value \(J_{\mathrm{ref}}=1.6031368\), which is reported in \cite{non-flow}.

\subsection{Implementation}
\label{subsec:cylinder-mixed-treatment}

For the problem, pressure is a Lagrange multiplier for incompressibility. The semidiscrete system is a differential--algebraic equation with singular mixed mass operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M=
 \begin{pmatrix}M_u&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:cylinder-dae-mass}
\end{equation}
Only velocity is propagated between time slabs.
Following the mixed-order setting of~\cite{non-flow}, on each slab \(I_n=(t_{n-1},t_n)\) the primal spatial pair is the inf--sup stable continuous-Galerkin Taylor--Hood element. The velocity and pressure space can be denoted as 
\begin{equation}
 V_{h,n}=[\mathrm{cG}(2;\mathcal T_n)]^2,
 \qquad Q_{h,n}=\mathrm{cG}(1;\mathcal T_n)\cap L^2_0(\Omega),
 \label{eq:cylinder-taylor-hood}
\end{equation}
and the primal uses \(\mathrm{dG}(1)\) in time. Velocity and pressure are solved together. The resulting velocity is discretely incompressible:
\begin{equation}
 (\nabla\!\cdot\boldsymbol u_h,q_h)_\Omega=0
 \qquad\forall q_h\in Q_{h,n},
 \label{eq:cylinder-discrete-divergence}
\end{equation}
but not exactly incompressible.

The enriched spaces used for the high-order adjoint are the continuous-Galerkin pair \(\mathrm{cG}3\otimes\mathrm{cG}2\),
% \pef{The natural enrichment would be to increase both polynomial degrees (for velocity and pressure) by one. The important point is that a differential operator applied to velocity maps you to a pressure, so their difference being one is the important thing, not the ratio between them.}
\begin{equation}
 V^+_{h,n}=[\mathrm{cG}(3;\mathcal T_n)]^2,
 \qquad Q^+_{h,n}=\mathrm{cG}(2;\mathcal T_n)\cap L^2_0(\Omega),
 \qquad \mathrm{dG}(2)\text{ in time}.
 \label{eq:cylinder-enriched-space}
\end{equation}

From our slabwise refinement strategy, spatial meshes may change from one slab to the next. A direct nodal interpolation of the outgoing velocity generally does not preserve the discretely divergence-free subspace associated with the receiving Taylor--Hood pair. Following the divergence-free transfer strategy described in \cite{projection} and its implementation in \cite{non-flow}, we define the transfer between consecutive time slabs as follows. Let
\[
V_{h,n,0}
:=
\left\{
\boldsymbol v_h\in V_{h,n}:
\boldsymbol v_h=\boldsymbol 0
\text{ on }\Gamma_D
\right\},
\]
and let \(V_{h,n,g}\) denote the affine velocity space satisfying the prescribed Dirichlet data \(g\). We first transfer the outgoing velocity trace to the new mesh by defining
\[
\boldsymbol{s}_n:=I_n\boldsymbol{u}^-,
\qquad
I_n:V_{h,n-1}\to V_{h,n}.
\]
The interpolated velocity \(\boldsymbol{s}_n\) is then projected onto the discretely divergence-free subset of \(V_{h,n,g}\). Specifically, we find
\[
\bigl(P_n\boldsymbol{u}^-,\lambda_n\bigr)
\in V_{h,n,g}\times Q_{h,n}
\]
such that
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
\bigl(P_n\boldsymbol{u}^-,\boldsymbol v_n\bigr)_\Omega
-
\bigl(\lambda_n,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol v_n\bigr)_\Omega
&=
\bigl(\boldsymbol{s}_n,\boldsymbol v_n\bigr)_\Omega
&&
\forall \boldsymbol v_n\in V_{h,n,0},\\
\bigl(\nabla\cdot P_n\boldsymbol{u}^-,q_n\bigr)_\Omega
&=0
&&
\forall q_n\in Q_{h,n}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here, \(\lambda_n\) is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the discrete divergence constraint. Let \(Q_{n,\mathrm{df}}^{L^2}\) denote the constrained \(L^2\)-projection onto the discretely divergence-free target set. When this projection is activated, the complete transfer is defined by
\begin{equation}
P_n\boldsymbol{u}^-
:=
Q_{n,\mathrm{df}}^{L^2}
\bigl(I_n\boldsymbol{u}^-\bigr).
\end{equation}
