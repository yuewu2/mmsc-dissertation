\section{Marking and Refinement}
\label{sec:marking-refinement}

Let \(\eta_{K,n}\) denote the signed indicator associated with the spatial cell \(K\in\mathcal T_n\) on the time slab \(I_n\). Each pair \((K,n)\) is treated as one space--time marking entity. Under the default marking score, its non-negative activity is defined by
\begin{equation}
  s_{K,n}:=|\eta_{K,n}|,
  \qquad
  S:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal T_n}s_{K,n}.
  \label{eq:marking-activity}
\end{equation}

A single global D\"orfler-type bulk marking operation \cite{Dorfler1996} is applied to all space--time cell--slab pairs. The activities from all slabs are concatenated and sorted in non-increasing order. The marking set \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{st}}\) is the smallest descending prefix whose cumulative activity reaches the prescribed fraction \(\theta\):
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{(K,n)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{st}}}s_{K,n}
  \ge
  \theta S.
  \label{eq:space-time-dorfler}
\end{equation}

The global marking set is then restricted to each time slab:
\begin{equation}
  M_n
  :=
  \left\{
    K\in\mathcal T_n:
    (K,n)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{st}}
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:slabwise-marked-cells}
\end{equation}
Under the default independent-slab policy, only the cells in \(M_n\) are refined on \(I_n\):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{new}}
  :=
  \operatorname{refine}(\mathcal T_n,M_n).
  \label{eq:slabwise-spatial-refinement}
\end{equation}

Temporal refinement is then controlled by a separate concentration trigger. A slab containing at least one marked cell is bisected when
\begin{equation}
  \frac{|M_n|}{|\mathcal T_n|}
  \ge
  \varphi_t.
  % \qquad
  % \varphi_t=0.05
  % \quad\text{by default}.
  \label{eq:within-slab-time-trigger}
\end{equation}
When \(I_n\) is bisected, both temporal children inherit \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{new}}\).

The complete procedure therefore consists of one global space--time bulk selection, followed by slab-local spatial refinement and a separate slabwise decision on temporal bisection.

\section{Automated Adaptive Algorithm}

The complete solve--estimate--mark--refine cycle is summarised in Algorithm~\ref{alg:complete-adaptive-cycle}.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Goal-oriented slabwise space--time adaptive cycle}
\label{alg:complete-adaptive-cycle}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require PDE, initial slab meshes, tolerance \(\mathrm{TOL}\), goal functional \(J\)
% \Ensure Adapted space--time discretisation and DWR estimate

\While{the stopping criterion is not satisfied}
  \State Build \(V_n\), the enriched spaces, \(P_n\), and \(P_n^\ast\) on the current slab meshes.
  \State Solve the primal problem forward and retain every dG slab polynomial.
  \If{\cref{eq:practical-nonlinear-estimator} is used}
    \State Solve the enriched primal problem.
  \EndIf
  \State Solve the low-order and enriched adjoints backward, differentiating the PDE and the actual terminal/running goal functional, and applying \(P_n^\ast\) at mesh changes.
  \State Form \(z^\star=z_h^+-z_h\), or optionally \(z^\star=z_h^+-I_hz_h^+\).
  \State Assemble the independent global DWR estimator.
  \State Recover volume, spatial-facet, temporal-face, and, when nonzero, mixed-ridge residuals in hierarchical order.
  \State Weight the recovered entities, assemble indicators \(\eta_{K,n}\), and record \(\eta_{\mathrm{loc}}\).
  \If{\(|\eta_{\mathrm{glob}}|\le\mathrm{TOL}\)}
    \State \Return the current discretisation and estimators.
  \EndIf
  \State Apply the marking criteria \eqref{eq:space-time-dorfler} and \eqref{eq:within-slab-time-trigger}.
  \State Refine the selected slab meshes, bisect selected time intervals, and rebuild the transfer operators.
\EndWhile

\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Primal, Goal, and Adjoint Solves}
\label{sec:primal-goal-adjoint-solves}

The time-dependent problems are supplied as semidiscrete UFL residuals. Firedrake provides the spatial finite-element spaces and variational solvers, while Irksome's \texttt{TimeStepper} uses these residuals to construct and solve the coupled stage systems associated with the chosen discontinuous Galerkin time discretisation \cite{andrews2026automatedgalerkintimestepping}.

Most of the experiments use an explicit UFL-generated adjoint implementation. In compact form, the discrete adjoint satisfies
\[
  \mathcal F_h'(U_h)^*Z_h=J_h'(U_h).
\]
UFL \texttt{derivative} constructs the linearisation of the primal residual, \texttt{adjoint} transposes the resulting bilinear form, and \texttt{action} inserts the current adjoint coefficient. For nonlinear problems, the resulting form is evaluated at the saved primal polynomial at the corresponding physical time. The common solver supplies the reverse-time mass term and solves the resulting linear problem slab by slab using the same Irksome dG machinery.

Terminal and running contributions to the goal functional are handled separately because they enter the reverse-time problem differently. The derivative of a terminal goal determines the final adjoint state, whose sensitivity is subsequently propagated through all preceding slabs. The derivative of a running goal instead acts as a source throughout the backward solve. Both derivatives are generated symbolically from the user-supplied UFL goal forms.

A fully automated alternative is provided by \texttt{firedrake-adjoint}, following the high-level discrete-adjoint methodology of \cite{Farrell_Ham_Funke_Rognes_2013} and implemented using Pyadjoint \cite{Mitusch2019}. It records the forward operations on a tape and traverses them in reverse to generate the corresponding discrete adjoint. This removes the need to write an explicit reverse-time solver.

The current implementation retains the complete forward trajectory in memory. For longer simulations, checkpointing could store only selected forward states and recompute intermediate states during the reverse traversal, reducing memory consumption at the cost of additional forward computation.

\subsection{Residual Localisation}

The primal residual is localised using a hierarchical bubble--cone recovery. Firedrake's scalar Bubble (\texttt{B}) and FacetBubble (\texttt{FB}) element families provide the auxiliary cell-interior and facet-supported test functions, respectively \cite{Firedrake}.
Temporal integration is performed using Gauss--Legendre quadrature, while the spatial forms are assembled by Firedrake using an explicitly selected quadrature degree.

\subsection{Transfer on Nonmatching Slab Meshes}
\label{sec:slab-transfer-adjoint}

Following the refinement strategy in Section~\ref{sec:marking-refinement}, consecutive time slabs may use different spatial meshes and finite-element spaces. The outgoing primal trace from \(I_{n-1}\) must therefore be transferred into \(V_n\). We denote the resulting linear interpolation operator by
\begin{equation}
  P_{n-1}:V_{n-1}\longrightarrow V_n
  \label{eq:forward-slab-transfer}
\end{equation}
and define the transferred dG interface defect by
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{n-1}(U_h)
  =
  U_h(t_{n-1}^{+})
  -
  P_{n-1}U_h(t_{n-1}^{-}).
  \label{eq:transferred-interface-defect-full}
\end{equation}

The reverse propagation is determined by the same discrete forward operator. Let \(P_{n-1}^{\ast}:V_n\rightarrow V_{n-1}\) denote its adjoint with respect to the time-mass pairings:
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(P_{n-1}w,z_n\bigr)_{M_n}
  =
  \bigl(w,P_{n-1}^{\ast}z_n\bigr)_{M_{n-1}}
  \qquad
  \forall w\in V_{n-1},\quad z_n\in V_n.
  \label{eq:mass-adjoint-transfer-definition}
\end{equation}
In coefficient form,
\begin{equation}
  P_{n-1}^{\ast}
  =
  M_{n-1}^{-1}P_{n-1}^{T}M_n.
  \label{eq:mass-adjoint-transfer-matrix}
\end{equation}

In the implementation, Firedrake assembles \(P_{n-1}\) as a cross-mesh finite-element interpolation operator. The forward trace is obtained by applying this matrix, while the reverse transfer is evaluated by a target mass action, a transpose matrix action, and a source mass solve as stated in ~\eqref{eq:mass-adjoint-transfer-matrix}.
